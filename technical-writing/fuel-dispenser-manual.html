The brief The source document explains fuel dispenser use, attendant safety, incident response and daily station routines. Its information is operationally important, so the redesign needed to improve navigation and visual hierarchy without changing the technical meaning or inventing product-specific details.

Design decisions Reorganised around user tasks The fueling procedure is grouped into a sequence of meaningful actions rather than presented as a long undifferentiated list.

Added a system-level visual A simplified diagram shows the fuel path and separates the underground tank, dispenser and vehicle tank. It is explicitly labelled conceptual.

Introduced visual safety hierarchy Warnings and operational cautions use consistent colour, spacing and headings to make important information easier to find.

Converted routines into checklists Opening, during-operation and closing activities are grouped into separate checklists to support scanning.

Made source limits visible The redesign states that maintenance, repair and installation are outside the source manual’s scope and that expert validation is required before operational use.

Accessibility and usability considerations • Consistent heading levels support navigation and scanning.

  • Short action statements and clear step numbering support procedural use.
  • Colour is paired with text labels; meaning does not rely on colour alone.
  • Checklist items are phrased as observable actions where the source supports them.
  • Final digital publication should use tagged headings, meaningful reading order, sufficient contrast and text
  • alternatives for diagrams.

Validation plan No usability outcomes are claimed because no user testing has been conducted for this sample. Before release, validate it with a subject-matter expert and representative readers. Check whether readers can identify the correct fuel type, locate the zero-display check, find the action for a mismatch, explain how to respond to a spill, and complete the daily checks without missing source-critical instructions.

Scope and integrity This case study is based on the supplied Fuel Dispenser User Manual. The redesign does not claim manufacturer approval, product-specific accuracy beyond the source, or measured performance improvements. The diagrams are conceptual; a qualified reviewer must approve any operational release.

Portfolio positioning This project can demonstrate procedural writing, audience analysis, information architecture, visual technical communication, safety-content prioritisation and source-controlled redesign.
