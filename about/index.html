How I approach the work

I start by identifying what a person needs to understand or accomplish. From there, I organise information around the user's context, make the sequence of ideas or actions explicit, and choose a format that fits the task.

In technical writing, that means making procedures, boundaries, and safety information easy to locate and follow. In learning design, it means connecting the intended outcome to practice and assessment. In product work, it means making assumptions, requirements, flows, and open questions visible to the people who need to make decisions.

Areas of interest

  • Technical communication and task-oriented documentation
  • Learning and development, instructional design, and virtual learning
  • Product management and evidence-led product artefacts
  • Question-Driven Documentation and AI-enabled knowledge experiences
  • Interactive and gamified learning

What this portfolio is designed to show

The portfolio prioritises the artefact and the thinking behind it. Where appropriate, each project will explain its purpose, audience, scope, structural choices, and limitations. It will distinguish between completed work, concepts in development, and planned additions.
